1. Titles & meta descriptions

Why it matters

Titles and meta descriptions are the primary snippets search engines show in results. Weak, missing, or duplicated copy reduces click-through and makes pages harder to distinguish in the index.

How to fix

Give every indexable template a unique <title> and meta description, keep lengths in a readable range (~30–60 / ~70–155 chars), and wire CMS or front-matter fields so pages are not stuck on empty defaults.

2. Crawlability & indexability

Why it matters

If crawlers cannot fetch or are told not to index important URLs, rankings and discovery fail regardless of on-page quality.

How to fix

Ship a production robots.txt that does not blanket-disallow the site, publish an XML sitemap (and reference it with Sitemap:), keep noindex only on intentional pages, and exclude drafts from the live sitemap.

3. Canonical URLs

Why it matters

Canonicals consolidate duplicate URL variants (www/apex, trailing slash, parameters) so ranking signals and indexing land on one preferred URL.

How to fix

Emit a self-referencing rel=canonical on page templates, align baseURL / site config with the live host, and keep host and trailing-slash policy consistent across links and tags.

4. Mobile-first basics

Why it matters

Google indexes with a mobile-first crawler. Broken viewports or desktop-only shells hurt usability signals and how content is understood on small screens.

How to fix

Include meta name="viewport", use responsive layout for main content, and provide usable mobile navigation with adequate touch targets.

5. Performance / CWV readiness

Why it matters

Slow LCP, layout shift, and blocked rendering reduce engagement and can weigh against rankings. Lab CWV from PSI/Unlighthouse makes the gaps concrete.

How to fix

Do not lazy-load the LCP image, compress oversized assets, trim heavy head scripts, load fonts without FOIT where possible, and attach PSI when available to track Performance / CWV scores.

6. HTTPS / host

Why it matters

Secure HTTPS is a baseline trust and ranking expectation. Mixed content and insecure absolute URLs break assets and confuse crawlers.

How to fix

Serve the production site on https, make absolute asset and OG URLs https, and avoid protocol-relative // resource URLs.

7. Semantic HTML & headings

Why it matters

Clear document language, a single primary H1, and logical heading hierarchy help crawlers and assistive tech understand page structure and topic.

How to fix

Set lang on <html>, use one H1 per page type, keep heading order sensible, and include <main> plus header/nav landmarks.

8. Image SEO

Why it matters

Alt text makes images understandable for search and accessibility. Missing alts lose Image Search relevance and create accessibility failures.

How to fix

Ensure image components support alt, write meaningful alts for content images, use empty alt for decorative images, and prefer modern formats (WebP/AVIF) when the pipeline allows.

9. JSON-LD structured data

Why it matters

JSON-LD clarifies entities (Organization, WebSite, Article, breadcrumbs) for rich results and machine understanding. Crawlers often miss this if you only look at HTML text.

How to fix

Add valid application/ld+json that matches visible content—at least Organization or WebSite sitewide, Article on article templates, and BreadcrumbList when the IA is multi-level.

10. Clean URL structure

Why it matters

Readable, stable URLs and fewer redirect hops preserve crawl budget and link equity and make sharing clearer.

How to fix

Use readable slugs, limit alias sprawl, point internal links at final URLs (not redirect chains), and avoid unnecessary query-param variants in primary navigation.

11. Internal linking

Why it matters

Internal links distribute authority, help discovery of key pages, and give crawlers meaningful anchor context.

How to fix

Link money pages from primary nav, cover utilities in footer/secondary nav, use accessible anchor text (not empty links), and reduce obvious orphans where detectable.

12. Open Graph / Twitter cards

Why it matters

OG and Twitter tags control how URLs look when shared on social and chat surfaces—often the first brand impression outside SERPs.

How to fix

Set og:title, og:description, and og:image (page or site default), plus og:url / og:type and twitter:card, keeping Twitter fields aligned with OG.

13. Status codes & error UX

Why it matters

Correct status codes and a real 404 experience prevent soft-404s, wasted crawl budget, and dead ends for users and bots.

How to fix

Ship a custom 404 (or framework not-found) page, avoid thin error content returning 200, and triage endemic internal 4xx / broken links from crawl exports.

14. International / hreflang

Why it matters

On multilingual sites, hreflang tells search engines which language/region variant to show and reduces duplicate-content confusion across locales.

How to fix

If multilingual: implement reciprocal hreflang (or equivalent) and x-default. If single-language: mark these checks n/a and avoid bogus hreflang in the sitemap.

15. Crawl-budget polish

Why it matters

Low-value taxonomies, facet URLs, and noisy prefetch patterns waste crawl budget that should go to money pages.

How to fix

Noindex or disable thin tags/taxonomies, avoid heavily linking irrelevant filters, and do not abuse preconnect/prefetch on every third party.

Attachment-driven / SF extras

Why it matters

Screaming Frog Issues and PageSpeed exports are independent evidence. Leaving Critical/High crawl issues or poor PSI scores unexplained means the audit ignored measured reality.

How to fix

When SF/PSI attachments are present, map Critical/High issues into matching rules, triage duplicates/alts/thin pages, and document Performance + SEO category scores and CWV lab metrics in Evidence. Without attachments, mark these checks unknown or n/a as noted in rules.md.
