1. AI crawler access

Why it matters

If search and citation bots cannot fetch your pages, answer engines will not cite you—content quality never gets a chance.

How to fix

Ship a production robots.txt that does not blanket-block AI search agents (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot/ClaudeBot as applicable). Document intentional training opt-outs (e.g. GPTBot) separately from search access, and declare Sitemap:.

2. Answer-shaped content

Why it matters

Answer engines lift short, self-contained openings. Pages that bury the point under marketing fluff are harder to quote.

How to fix

Lead key pages with a direct answer (roughly 40–80 words), use question-shaped headings where intent is informational, and add FAQ blocks when multiple related questions are answered on-page.

3. Extractable structure

Why it matters

Clean heading hierarchy and quotable passages make extraction reliable. Chaotic skips and vague pronouns reduce citability.

How to fix

Use one H1 per page type, keep H1→H2→H3 order sensible, write self-contained passages, and prefer lists/tables for steps and comparisons.

4. FAQ / HowTo schema

Why it matters

FAQPage and HowTo JSON-LD help machines identify answer-eligible blocks when they match visible content. Skip when the page is not Q&A-shaped.

How to fix

When FAQ/Q&A is visible, add valid FAQPage JSON-LD that mirrors on-page pairs. Add HowTo only for real step procedures. Prefer n/a over fail when intent does not match.

5. Entity & brand clarity

Why it matters

Models need a clear “who” to attribute. Ambiguous brand/product naming and missing Organization markup weaken citations.

How to fix

Keep brand naming consistent, ship Organization/WebSite JSON-LD, expand acronyms on first use, and link Person entities where authorship is claimed.

6. E-E-A-T / trust signals

Why it matters

Answer engines prefer sources that look accountable—named authors, credentials, and verifiable claims.

How to fix

Show author or org attribution on article/docs templates, link About/Contact, and avoid unsourced authority claims.

7. Freshness

Why it matters

Recency is a common citation signal for changing topics. Stale cornerstone pages lose share of voice over time.

How to fix

Show publish/updated dates on time-sensitive content, set datePublished/dateModified in Article schema when used, and refresh flagship pages periodically.

8. Citation readiness

Why it matters

Research (GEO / Aggarwal et al.) finds statistics, quotations, and cite-sources style content lift visibility in generative answers.

How to fix

Add verifiable facts and outbound proof links, include quotable attributions where helpful, and ship original examples or data on flagship pages—avoid keyword stuffing.

9. Comparison & decision content

Why it matters

“Best / vs / which” prompts need criteria and trade-offs. Pure docs pages can mark this area n/a.

How to fix

For comparative intent: state criteria, use tables or explicit trade-offs, name alternatives fairly, and clarify audience fit. Otherwise score n/a.

10. Attachments & measurement

Why it matters

Answerlint JSON/CSV (and optional llms lint / prompt packs) are independent evidence—like Screaming Frog for SEO. Unmapped attachment fails leave measured gaps unexplained.

How to fix

When Answerlint is attached, map fail/warn checks into areas 1–9 and record scores in Evidence. Handle llms lint and prompt-pack results when provided; otherwise use unknown or n/a as noted in rules.md.
